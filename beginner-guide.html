Beginner Resources

Your First Bachata Class

Starting a new hobby can be exciting. Here is everything you need to know to feel confident, meet new people, and enjoy getting fit on the dance floor.

  • No partner needed
  • No experience required
  • Classes taught in English
01 What to Expect 02 Dance Shoes 03 Roles 04 Etiquette
01

What to Expect

Walking into a dance studio for the first time can be intimidating, but finding a new passion should not be. Dancing is a fantastic way to stay active and connect with people outside your usual circle. When you feel ready, check the weekly Bachata schedule.

What to Wear

Comfort is key! Wear clothes that allow you to move freely. Gym clothes, leggings, or casual wear like jeans are fine. Avoid anything too restrictive.

Read Full Guide →

Partner Rotation

In our classes, we highly suggest rotating partners. It helps you learn faster and meet new people. If you prefer to stay with your own partner, we respect that choice.

02

Shoe Guide

Having the right tools makes a huge difference. Protect your knees and turn smoothly.

Best Choice

Dance Sneakers

There are brands specialized in sneaker dancing shoes. These are highly recommended for leaders and followers. Ask us for a promo code.

1
Great Start

Comfortable Sneakers

Perfect for your first class! Any comfortable indoor sneaker works.

2
Not Recommended

Street Shoes

Dirty soles damage the studio floor. Please always bring a separate pair of clean indoor shoes.

03

Roles

Bachata is a conversation between two people. Nowadays, anyone is welcome to choose either role.

Leader

Guides the dance and suggests moves. They are responsible for navigation and safety.

Follower

Interprets the signals and adds their own style.

04

Etiquette & Hygiene

Dancing is a close-contact activity. Please be mindful of your hygiene.

  • Fresh breath — mints are great.
  • Deodorant and a fresh shirt go a long way.
  • Please keep phones away during class so everyone can stay present and focused.
  • Please no chewing gum for safety and respect for teachers and other attendants.
