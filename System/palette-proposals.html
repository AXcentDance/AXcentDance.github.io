AXcent Dance — Palette Proposals

Round 3 — the night glamour family. The owner shortlisted R1·01 (Midnight & Ivory), R2·05 (Noir & Brass) and R2·03 (Night & Mist). Their shared DNA: a dark nocturnal canvas, one elegant counterpoint (ivory, metal, or mist), and a single hot accent. Round 3 explores four more palettes built on exactly that feeling. Rounds 2 and 1 are kept below for reference. Click any swatch to copy its hex. Every palette now also shows the studio logo (a vector re-draw of its shapes — dancers, AX, script, divider) recolored in that palette, on both main canvases.

No green (competitor) No purple Dual main colors One strong accent WCAG AA contrast Premium, warm, beginner-friendly

How these were chosen

  1. 60-30-10 hierarchy: the dominant main carries ~60% of the page, the second main ~30% (bands, cards, contrast sections), and the accent stays scarce (~10%) so CTAs always win attention.
  2. Dual mains = two canvases: both mains take turns as backgrounds (dark hero / light band, or vice versa), which gives the site rhythm without adding more hues.
  3. Complementary temperature: pairing a cool or neutral main with a warm main (or warm accent) reads as "trust + excitement" — the classic blue/orange logic adapted per palette.
  4. Psychology for a bachata studio: warm reds/corals signal passion and energy; deep dark canvases signal premium; creams keep it human and welcoming for nervous beginners.
  5. Ownability: local dance competitors cluster around black/red, neon, and (one) green. Each proposal stakes out ground none of them holds.
  6. Accessibility: body text ≥ 7:1 on its canvas, CTA labels ≥ 4.5:1 on their fill (noted per palette).

Psychology & rationale

Rose-sand is skin-warm and modern (fashion and beauty language — strong pull for the styling / lady-styling audience), slate grounds it with cool professional weight, and the crimson accent is undiluted bachata passion. The dual mains double as the text system: slate IS the text color on rose, cream is the text on slate — maximum coherence with a minimal token set. The most contemporary and Instagram-native of the five.

Contrast (WCAG)

Slate on rose-sand 9.6:1 AAACream on slate ~12:1 AAAWhite on crimson CTA 5.0:1 AACrimson as large text on rose AA-Large

The only palette here where white-on-accent passes AA — crimson buttons can carry white labels.
